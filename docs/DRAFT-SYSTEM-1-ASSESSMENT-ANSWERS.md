## Question 1: What are your distinct operational units?

### Answer:

Lithodat currently operates **three primary operational units** with a fourth in planning:

**1. LithoSurfer (Front-End Platform)**
- **Description:** Core software product for geological data management and visualization
- **Current State:** Operational and central to business strategy
- **Outputs:** Software platform for exploration companies to manage, visualize, and analyze geological data
- **Customers:** Exploration companies, mining companies, geological consultants
- **Key Strength:** Fully functional with all core capabilities; proven product-market fit
- **Strategic Role:** Customer acquisition vehicle and long-term subscription revenue stream

**2. LithoBuild (Custom Development)**
- **Description:** Software development consulting and custom tool creation for clients
- **Current State:** Primary historical revenue generator
- **Outputs:** Custom software solutions, integrations, specialized tools for client-specific needs
- **Customers:** Mining companies, exploration firms, labs with unique technical requirements
- **Strategic Role:** Bootstrap capital generator for next 1-2 years to fund Surfer and Data development
- **Leadership Consensus:** Temporary necessity, not core long-term business

**3. LithoData (Back-End Data Services)**
- **Description:** Data collection, cleaning, curation, and product development
- **Sub-components:**
  - **LithoClean:** Data cleaning and quality assurance
  - **LithoMine:** Data extraction from academic reports and industry sources
- **Outputs:** Curated geological and geochemical datasets, clean data products
- **Customers:** Surfer users, exploration companies, researchers
- **Strategic Role:** Future core differentiation; private data aggregation at scale
- **Key Opportunity:** Close the "knowledge gap" by aggregating data faster than competitors

**4. LithoAI (Future/Emerging)**
- **Description:** AI-powered prospectivity analysis and integrated intelligence tools
- **Current State:** Planned capabilities, not yet operational as distinct unit
- **Potential Outputs:** Prospectivity maps, AI-driven exploration recommendations, predictive analytics
- **Strategic Role:** Premium offering layer on top of Surfer + Data
- **Timeline:** Development contingent on Surfer customer base and data volume reaching critical mass

### Operational Unit Boundaries:

**Clear Boundaries:**
- Surfer is product development and delivery
- Build is client services and custom work
- Data is data operations (collection, cleaning, curation)

**Gray Areas (Current Challenges):**
- Some staff work across multiple units (e.g., Nora works on both Surfer and Data)
- Task categorization by system sometimes unclear
- Resource allocation is ad-hoc rather than systematic
- Quality control well-defined for Build, less clear for Surfer/Data

## Question 4: Do operational units have control over their own resources?

### Answer:

**Limited control - resources are centrally managed**

**Current Resource Control Model:**

**LithoSurfer:**
- ❌ **No dedicated budget:** Doesn't control spending decisions
- ⚠️ **Partial people control:** Juan leads dev priorities, but doesn't allocate hours across team
- ❌ **No hiring authority:** Can't independently hire Surfer-dedicated developers
- ✅ **Technical decisions:** Can choose tech stack, architecture within Surfer scope
- **Resource Control Score: 40%**

**LithoBuild:**
- ⚠️ **Informal budget:** Controls project delivery, but no formal budget allocation
- ⚠️ **People allocation:** Can request developers for projects, but doesn't control availability
- ✅ **Client decisions:** Can accept/decline projects based on capacity
- ✅ **Delivery autonomy:** Controls how projects are delivered (methodology, tools)
- **Resource Control Score: 60%**

**LithoData:**
- ❌ **No budget:** No spending authority
- ❌ **No dedicated team:** People shared across units (Kimberly, Nora split time)
- ❌ **No hiring authority:** Can't hire data specialists independently
- ⚠️ **Priority influence:** Can advocate for priorities, but doesn't control roadmap
- **Resource Control Score: 20%**

---

### Resource Types & Control:

**1. People (Human Resources):**
- **Current:** Centrally managed by directors, staff assigned to projects/units as needed
- **Challenge:** "Staff work across multiple systems" - unclear primary unit ownership
- **Example:** Kimberly asks Wayne instead of Juan (unclear reporting lines)
- **Needed:** Clear allocation percentages, primary unit ownership, decision authority

**2. Budget (Financial Resources):**
- **Current:** Xero has financial data, but no dashboards by unit
- **Challenge:** No transparency on unit profitability, cost allocation unclear
- **Example:** Unknown if Surfer is profitable or subsidized by Build
- **Needed:** Unit-level P&Ls, allocated budgets for hiring/tools/marketing

**3. Tools & Technology:**
- **Current:** Shared infrastructure (Jira, GitHub, Google Workspace)
- **Control:** Directors approve major tool purchases
- **Challenge:** Ad-hoc tool decisions, sometimes redundant tools
- **Needed:** Clear tool ownership, budget for unit-specific tools

**4. Decision-Making Authority:**
- **Current:** Many operational decisions escalate to directors
- **Challenge:** "Communication sometimes goes up-then-across, not horizontal"
- **Example:** Kimberly should ask Juan (unit leader), instead asks Wayne (director)
- **Needed:** Clear RACI (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) for decision types

**5. Time Allocation:**
- **Current:** Ad-hoc scheduling, no systematic capacity planning
- **Challenge:** Unclear which unit has priority when conflicts arise
- **Example:** Should Nora prioritize Surfer feature or Data cleaning task?
- **Needed:** Resource allocation transparency, priority frameworks

---

### Implications of Limited Resource Control:

**Positive:**
- ✅ Flexible resource sharing across units (can help each other)
- ✅ Prevents sub-optimization (units don't hoard resources)
- ✅ Directors can reallocate quickly based on strategic priorities

**Negative:**
- ❌ Delayed decisions (everything escalates to directors)
- ❌ Unclear accountability (who's responsible for unit success?)
- ❌ Limited autonomy (reduces innovation and ownership)
- ❌ Scaling challenge ("Current model worked for 3 directors, doesn't scale to 14-25 people")

---

### Recommendations for Improved Resource Control:

**Level 1: Visibility (Do This First)**
1. **Resource Dashboard:** Show current allocation by unit
   - Who works on what % of time
   - Budget spent by unit (even if directors still approve)
   - Tool costs allocated to units

**Level 2: Influence (Gradual Transition)**
2. **Budget Proposals:** Units propose quarterly budgets (directors approve)
3. **Hiring Input:** Unit leaders participate in hiring, even if directors decide
4. **Priority Setting:** Units set own priorities (aligned to company OKRs)

**Level 3: Authority (Mature State)**
4. **Delegated Budgets:** Units control spending up to limits (e.g., <$5K autonomous)
5. **Hiring Authority:** Unit leaders can hire (within approved headcount)
6. **Tool Selection:** Units choose own tools (within budget, shared for common needs)

**Key Principle:** Autonomy with transparency
- Units should have more control, BUT
- All decisions visible to leadership
- Company-level OKRs guide unit decisions
- Directors retain veto power on strategic calls

### Coordination Problems Identified:

**Problem 1: Communication Goes Up-Then-Across (Not Horizontal)**
- **Example:** "Staff unsure who to ask for what (e.g., Kimberly asking Wayne instead of Juan)"
- **Why it happens:**
  - Unclear unit boundaries
  - Reporting lines not clear
  - Directors always available, so easier to ask them
- **Impact:**
  - Slows decisions (directors become bottleneck)
  - Reduces unit leader authority (Juan should be first point of contact for Surfer questions)
  - Doesn't scale (directors can't be in all coordination conversations at 25 people)

**Problem 2: Multiple Signal Groups Without Clear Structure**
- **Challenge:** Which group for which topic?
- **Impact:**
  - Information scattered
  - People miss important updates
  - Duplicate discussions
- **Needed:** Clear purpose per group, or consolidate

**Problem 3: Jira Usage Not Systematic Across Systems**
- **Challenge:** Some units use Jira religiously (Build), others less so (Data?)
- **Impact:**
  - Hard to see cross-unit dependencies
  - Build's work visible, Data's work invisible
  - Can't track cross-unit projects end-to-end
- **Needed:** Standardized Jira usage, or explicit decision to use different tools per unit

**Problem 4: No Formal Dependency Management**
- **Challenge:** When Surfer needs data from Data team, how is that tracked?
- **Impact:**
  - Surfer may wait on Data (blocks progress)
  - Data may not know Surfer urgency (misaligned priorities)
  - No visibility into blockers
- **Needed:** Explicit dependency tracking (could be in Jira, could be in another tool)

**Problem 5: Shared Resources Create Coordination Overhead**
- **Challenge:** Kimberly 50% Surfer, 50% Data (or some split)
- **Impact:**
  - Who manages Kimberly's priorities?
  - When conflict, who decides?
  - Overhead of context-switching between units
- **Needed:** Clear allocation, clear priority framework, or dedicate resources to single unit

---

### What Good Coordination Would Look Like:

**System 2 Mechanisms (VSM "Coordination" System):**
1. **Horizontal communication:** Units talk directly, not via directors
2. **Scheduling/timetabling:** Shared calendar, known capacity, clear commitments
3. **Standard operating procedures:** When A needs B, here's the process
4. **Conflict resolution:** When units disagree, clear escalation path (but shouldn't need often)
5. **Information sharing:** What does everyone need to know? Systematic broadcast

**Practical Examples:**
- **Surfer-Data handoff:** When Surfer needs new dataset:
  - Surfer creates Jira ticket with requirements
  - Data estimates and commits to timeline
  - Regular sync (weekly?) to track progress
  - Clear definition of done

- **Resource sharing:** When Build needs developer for Surfer project:
  - Request goes to Juan (Surfer), not directors
  - Juan and Build lead agree on allocation
  - Directors informed, not asked to decide

- **Priority conflicts:** When Surfer and Data both need Kimberly:
  - Default priority framework (e.g., customer-facing work wins)
  - If framework doesn't resolve, escalate to directors
  - Log conflict and pattern (if happens often, rethink structure)

### Recommendations:
