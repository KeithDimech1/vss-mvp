Executive Summary

GDAC-SA's Data Integration Challenge

The Saudi Geological Survey requires a transformational AI/Big Data Analytics Platform to support Vision 2030's objective of developing the mining sector as the Kingdom's third economic pillar.

This platform must integrate 50+ years of diverse geological data from the National Geological Database (NGD), National Information Center (NIC), and new field campaigns—including reports, geochemistry, geophysics, remote sensing, geology, and drilling records—then apply advanced AI analytics to deliver mineral prospectivity predictions, target generation, and exploratory forecasting.

The RFQ correctly identifies the fundamental challenge: much of this data exists with varying quality, incomplete metadata, inconsistent formats, and aggregations spanning decades. Phase 1 must create "the underlying data ecosystem" through data structure design, feature engineering, and algorithm development before AI can succeed.

Lithodat's Proven Solution

Lithodat Pty Ltd has already built exactly what GDAC-SA envisions. Over the past decade, we have successfully delivered three national-scale data cleaning and AI-ready infrastructure programs for federal government organizations across Australia and Canada:

  • EarthBank – National geochemistry platform for AuScope and Geoscience Australia
  • Isotopes.au – Federal stable isotope platform developed with CSIRO and four Australian government agencies
  • CATCH Database – Canadian Thermochronology Database for Natural Resources Canada

These platforms integrate the same type of multi-agency, multi-decade geological data that GDAC-SA must harmonize to power AI-enabled analytics for mineral exploration, environmental assessment, and geoscientific research.

Why Clean Data Is Critical for AI Success

The RFQ recognizes a critical principle that determines whether GDAC-SA succeeds or fails: the only pathway to AI-enabled mineral exploration workflows is through clean, standardized, FAIR data.

Machine learning models cannot overcome poor data quality. No amount of sophisticated AI algorithms can extract reliable mineral prospectivity predictions from inconsistent terminology, missing spatial coordinates, incompatible measurement units, or uncontrolled vocabularies.

Attempts to "bolt on" AI to poorly structured data inevitably fail because poor data requires more effort from AI and subsequently produces poor predictions. Clean data first is the foundation for AI success.

Lithodat's Five-Stage Clean Data Pipeline

Our proven five-stage pipeline has successfully powered three national platforms, and this exact architecture will deliver GDAC-SA's requirements:

Stage 1: Data Ingestion

Multi-format adapters handle Excel, CSV, legacy databases, and unstructured data with automated validation against geoscience vocabularies and quality scoring at point of entry. For CATCH Database, we digitized 50+ years of legacy data (1970-2021) from 91 disparate sources including analogue paper reports and non-vectorized PDFs—exactly the historical data challenge GDAC-SA faces with NGD and NIC archives.

Stage 2: Semantic Standardization

SKOS-based controlled vocabularies (ontologies) map diverse terminology to standardized concepts, enabling AI feature engineering across heterogeneous sources. For Isotopes.au we developed 250+ standardized field mappings validated across six institutional data sources from three federal agencies—directly analogous to harmonizing Saudi Geological Survey data across departments with different conventions.

Stage 3: FAIR-Compliant Database Storage

Comprehensive metadata capture, full audit trail, and RESTful APIs enable programmatic access by AI workflows. EarthBank achieved CoreTrustSeal certification, the international standard for trustworthy digital repositories. FAIR data is the technical foundation for AI success: Findability through rich metadata, Accessibility via APIs, Interoperability through controlled vocabularies, and Reusability with provenance tracking.

Stage 4: SGS Live Portal Development

Dual-access systems provide public-facing data portals for open geoscience access plus private secure areas for SGS internal workflows with role-based access control. EarthBank and Isotopes.au are fully operational public platforms serving geoscientists globally while maintaining secure administrative features for government requirements.

Stage 5: AI/ML Application Layer

Advanced analytics through feature engineering from clean structured data, machine learning model training for classification/regression/clustering, mineral prospectivity mapping and target generation, and time series analysis for trend detection.

Result: Phase 1's data ecosystem seamlessly feeds Phase 2's AI applications, with Phase 3-4 data additions automatically integrated through established quality pipelines—creating a continuously improving, evidence-based resource discovery platform for Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030.
