1. Overview

The CATCH database is Canada's first unified
national repository for low-temperature
thermochronology data, integrating 1,427 samples from 91 publications spanning
1970-2021. Developed by Lithodat Pty Ltd (Fabian Kohlmann, Malcolm McMillan)
with the Geological Survey of Canada (Dr. Jeremy Powell, Dr. Dawn Kellett),
CATCH demonstrates national-scale data standardization directly applicable to
GDAC-SA's multi-agency integration requirements.
Performance: $400,000 CAD, 18 months (October 2021 - March 2023), delivered on-time and
on-budget. Database scale: 996 fission-track datapoints, 762 (U-Th)/He
datapoints, 16,277 single grain measurements, published via DOI
10.4095/pdreqf905k and accessible through AusGeochem.

2. History & Funding

Natural Resources Canada's Geological Survey
(GSC) is Canada's national geoscience organization. Prior to CATCH, Canada had
no central thermochronology database despite decades of published
research—analogous to GDAC-SA's challenge of fragmented geological survey data.
In October 2021, GSC awarded Lithodat a
$400,000 CAD contract to design Canada's first national thermochronology data
model, harvest all published research (1970-2021), and integrate with AusGeochem's FAIR platform. The project required systematic
harmonization of legacy laboratory outputs from multiple provinces, automated
QA/QC pipeline development, and extensive legacy data digitization from
analogue paper reports and non-vectorized PDFs.
Deliverables included national data schema
accommodating four thermochronometer types
(apatite/zircon fission-track, apatite/zircon (U-Th)/He), standardization of 51
years of provincial data, complete harvesting from 91 publications, and
integration with international reporting standards.

4. Data Model & Structure

CATCH employs a purpose-built data model
accommodating four thermochronometer types
(Apatite/Zircon Fission Track, Apatite/Zircon (U-Th)/He) plus thermal history
modeling. Canada's first unified national thermochronology schema handles
complex kinetic parameters, accommodates legacy laboratory outputs, and
provides ML-compatible, standardized structure.

Figure 1: CATCH database northern Canada compilation showing 1,427

data (colored by geologic age: red = Precambrian, green = Paleozoic, blue =
Mesozoic, yellow = Cenozoic). Demonstrates national-scale geospatial data

Data Completeness: Single grain data preservation (16,277 measurements), fission-track length
capture (27,848 measurements) from digitized histograms, automated QA/QC
pipelines validated by GSC scientists, comprehensive provenance tracking,
georeferencing workflow, and thermal history modeling integration.

FAIR Principles: Findable via DOI (10.4095/pdreqf905k) in GSC Open File series; Accessible
through AusGeochem with no authentication barriers; Interoperable
using community-agreed schemas; Reusable with complete metadata and

On-Time, On-Budget: 18-month timeline met (October 2021 - March 2023), $400,000 CAD managed
with complete accountability, demonstrating reliable government partnership.
National-Scale Achievement: Standardized data across entire Canadian territory, harmonized provincial
inconsistencies, integrated 51 years of data (1970-2021), harvested all
published research from 91 sources—demonstrating systematic multi-decade
integration applicable to GDAC-SA.
Technical Excellence: Automated data ingestion for multiple formats (digital, analogue paper,
non-vectorized PDFs), QA/QC validation by GSC staff, and cloud-ready
infrastructure supporting advanced analytics.
