Nov 17, 2025
Lithodat Product Meeting Pt2
Invited Keith Dimech Fabian Kohlmann Vinko Novak Wayne Noble Moritz Theile
Attachments Lithodat Product Meeting Pt2 Notes by Gemini 
Meeting records Recording 


Summary
Keith Dimech and Moritz Theile agreed on the concept of a data marketplace but had extended discussions on the definition and naming of "private data" for different license tiers, with Fabian Kohlmann and Wayne Noble contributing insights on visibility, licensing, and suggested terminology. The participants discussed the need for strong incentives to encourage Pro and Enterprise license purchases, the current model for Premium data sales, and the necessity of a rigorous approval process and quality control standards for third-party commercial data sold on the marketplace, to which Fabian Kohlmann suggested a tiered commission structure. Vinko Novak, Wayne Noble, and Moritz Theile stressed the critical need for robust security, including SSO and certification, and comprehensive legal agreements to manage the significant risks associated with hosting sensitive private data. The team, including Keith Dimech, Wayne Noble, and Moritz Theile, concluded with plans for a March launch, agreeing on the need for standard contracts for marketplace data, ensuring QC workflows, and promoting subscriptions through Litho Surfer and a separate gallery with SSO for a seamless user experience.


Details
* Data Marketplace Concept Keith Dimech envisioned the data marketplace where users can log into Lithu, view available data, and purchase packages via a Litha subscription. Moritz Theile agreed with the concept, noting the idea was identical to theirs, but expressed concern about existing descriptions and naming, particularly the term "private data". Keith Dimech suggested catching up to clarify the difference between the current definition of "private data" and the one they described.
* Definition of Private Data Moritz Theile explained that in their current context, a private package is visible only to the creator and their added team members, unless the distribution is set to the institutional level, making it visible to all institutional users. Keith Dimech believed their understanding of private data was the same, describing it as private until the user shares it beyond their realm. Fabian Kohlmann clarified that "private data" includes a second kind, institutional private data, which is private within an organization like Anglo-American but not commercial or individual private data.
* Data Visibility and Licensing The group discussed the distinction between Lithu Pro and Lithu Enterprise licenses, where the latter offers the option to silo data off to projects within an enterprise, whereas the Pro account shares data with everyone in that institution. Moritz Theile suggested calling "private data" "institutional data sharing" to avoid confusion, but Keith Dimech preferred to stick to three external terms: private, commercial, and public data. Wayne Noble suggested that "private" could serve as an umbrella term, emphasizing that the discussion is about the visibility of data, not the data grouping itself.
* Private Data Visibility for Licenses Keith Dimech confirmed that if a user has a Pro license, every package they create will be private, but everyone in that institute will see every package, to which Moritz Theile corrected that as long as it is private, only the creator can see the data unless they share it or build a team. Moritz Theile detailed that with a free license, a user can only create private packages that cannot be shared, while a Pro license allows switching the package to institutional visibility. The team noted that they need to determine if a Pro license can restrict who a user brings into their team, clarifying that a Pro license allows sharing with anyone who has a Lithu login.
* Data Sharing and Incentives for Pro Licenses Keith Dimech suggested that for Pro licenses, there is a possibility to enforce sharing only among assigned license holders in the future, although it is not a current priority. They also discussed whether to keep free users' private data on Lithu and the incentive to upgrade to a Pro license, suggesting that the free version could restrict sharing private data to incentivize a Pro upgrade. The team discussed implementing a clause, similar to EarthBank's, which states that private data uploaded on the free version might become public after a certain time, such as two or four years.
* Incentivizing License Purchase Fabian Kohlmann raised a concern regarding junior companies with multiple employees who might only purchase a few Pro licenses, limiting data visibility for the remaining staff. Keith Dimech suggested that the incentive structure must be strong, emphasizing that multiple people viewing private data require additional licenses, possibly at a discounted rate.
* Premium Data Subscription Sales The discussion moved to the "Premium" data sales, which for the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) will continue to be handled through contracts and direct sales, similar to the current process with BHP. Keith Dimech outlined that the contract would cover data by type, region, and annual subscription price, with the global option being the ideal sale. Moritz Theile defined Premium data as purchasing a certain kind and location of data.
* "Premium Data" Naming Convention Keith Dimech clarified that "Premium" was used to differentiate their subscription data from "commercial marketplace data," which involves data sold by other entities and is package-based, not region- or type-based. Wayne Noble and Moritz Theile agreed that the term "premium" is not ideal because it suggests a higher quality, whereas all Lithu data should be considered high quality; they agreed to brainstorm a different term like "custom data" or "Lithodata subscriptions".
* Data Exchange and Package Selection Keith Dimech emphasized that in the marketplace, if a user selects data that spans multiple packages, they must buy the entire packages and cannot select individual points. Wayne Noble suggested the ability to create a "virtual package" or a constructed package from a user's chosen samples, even if they span multiple packages, to avoid forcing the purchase of unnecessary data.
* Quality Control for Commercial Data Keith Dimech raised the concern about ensuring the quality of data sold by third parties on the platform, suggesting that Lithu should have standards to prevent being associated with "crap data". Fabian Kohlmann proposed a tiered commission structure where the commission is higher if Lithu performs the data cleaning and conversion, but emphasized the need for quality control for all data uploaded. Keith Dimech stressed the necessity of a rigorous approval process for both the seller and the package to validate the data's authenticity, such as requiring a lab report or published paper.
* Litho Clean Service Pricing The team discussed pricing for the Litho Clean service for historic data, agreeing that a quote is necessary after a preliminary review of the data. Moritz Theile suggested offering a fixed-price analysis workshop for a rough overview and proposal of the cleaning progress, which would precede the actual quote.
* Security and Sensitive Data The team discussed security for hosting sensitive private data from mining companies, which Vinko Novak and Wayne Noble indicated should include single sign-on and being certified (like ISO certified) to ensure the environment is secure and trustworthy. Moritz Theile warned that a big company putting a multi-million-dollar dataset on the platform would make it a "honeypot," and the current system is not prepared for that level of security risk. Fabian Kohlmann highlighted the massive legal risk this creates for Lithu.
* Legal and Security Protections Keith Dimech noted the importance of having legal agreements in place, such as clauses addressing hacking and security breaches, and that this should be the first action item. The consensus was that Pro and Enterprise licenses' primary benefit is the ability to upload and manage private data, making strong security and legal protections crucial. They also discussed audit trails for tracking when data is switched between public and private.
* Commercial Data Sales Model Fabian Kohlmann introduced the idea that a commercial data supporter may want a one-off sale on the marketplace, not just a subscription. Keith Dimech agreed that the option for a single cost should be available, while Fabian Kohlmann suggested the one-off sale should be significantly more expensive than a subscription to encourage the latter. Wayne Noble cautioned that allowing data to be sold for a very low price could devalue the data on the platform, and prices would need to be approved.
* Perpetual Data Access and Download Risk The discussion turned to whether a one-off purchase grants perpetual access within the Lithosurfer Pro account or if a subscription is required for continued access, noting the technical aspects are not yet in place. The team acknowledged the risk of users subscribing, downloading all the data, and then canceling the subscription, highlighting that this is an inherent problem in selling downloadable data. Wayne Noble stated that technical solutions are limited, and legal agreements are necessary to protect Lithu from potential liability and data devaluing.
* Sustaining the Business Model Keith Dimech concluded that data downloading is a reality they must be prepared for, and the continued value of the Lithu platform lies in its ability to display seven data types in one spot and offer access to continually growing data through subscriptions. Moritz Theile emphasized that the leverage the platform has is ensuring the data pipelines are running and providing high-quality new data first.
* Legal and Contractual Considerations for Marketplace Data Keith Dimech introduced the need for a standard contract for marketplace data, including known price limits and the model (subscription or pay once). Moritz Theile and Fabian Kohlmann noted that private data should not automatically become public, especially if people are running experiments, to prevent confusion about the data's authenticity. They agreed on the necessity of Quality Control (QC) and verification workflows for any data made public, with Fabian Kohlmann stressing the importance of updating the Terms and Conditions (T&Cs) regardless of the existing Oscore or Earthbank T&Cs.
* Liability and Data Provision The team discussed liability, agreeing that they should not be responsible for promises made by third-party data sellers and that the data should be provided "as is". Fabian Kohlmann emphasized the need to strengthen the contract section regarding liability once data starts being uploaded, ensuring it covers all aspects of data provision. They also discussed commercial discussions needed for private data subscriptions (Pro and Enterprise) and data subscriptions before Wayne Noble speaks about them in January.
* Timing of Commercial Discussions and Product Launch Moritz Theile suggested that Wayne Noble only needs marketing material to generate interest, and the legal aspects do not have to be finalized for initial discussions. Wayne Noble confirmed they can speak about the Pro and Enterprise offerings even if they are not yet online, distinguishing between "talking" and "offering". They agreed that January is a good time to aim for testing concepts and having the website ready, but a more realistic launch date for turning everything on, including finalized contracts, would be very early March.
* Trial Data and New Data Notifications for Subscribers Keith Dimech proposed having a test version or trial data for the Pro version before public launch to ensure functionality and find bugs with real people. Moritz Theile raised the question of how to handle new data appearing for subscribers, and Keith Dimech confirmed that subscribers should get live data and that alerts via email or within the product would be beneficial for retention. Wayne Noble supported the idea of both email and in-product alerts for new records.
* Promoting Data Subscriptions on Litho Surfer Keith Dimech suggested that Litho Surfer should promote data subscriptions (premium data) by advertising the option to subscribe to more data and directing users to the gallery. Moritz Theile noted that this preview functionality is not on the current server portal but will be on the gallery, suggesting promotion should focus on the gallery. Wayne Noble concurred with providing an indication in Litho Surfer that directs users to the catalog.
* Single Sign-On and Application Distinction Moritz Theile highlighted Single Sign-On (SSO) as a necessary topic for switching easily between the Litho Surfer and the gallery, acknowledging that the gallery is a separate portal. Wayne Noble expressed concern about user confusion between the two applications, but Fabian Kohlmann and Keith Dimech agreed that a smooth User Experience (UX) with SSO is essential for seamless switching. Wayne Noble pointed out that the gallery needs to be accessible without sign-in for people to view the data available.
* Gallery and Data Purchase Workflow Moritz Theile clarified that the gallery is accessible without login for everyone to view data, covering both their subscriptions and marketplace data. When a user wants to buy data, they can request a quote, which triggers a manual process involving an offer and the data appearing in their account. Wayne Noble suggested that Litho Surfer subscriptions and marketplace data should ideally be available within one application, with different types of commercial data flagged accordingly.
* Automating Transactions for Smaller Sales Keith Dimech and Wayne Noble discussed the long-term goal of enabling automated payment services, such as Stripe, for smaller, impulse transactions. Moritz Theile agreed that the immediate goal for the gallery is to generate quotes, allowing for manual handling and learning before automating the payment process after approximately 100 quotes.
* Distinguishing Free and Commercial Data Fabian Kohlmann confirmed that subscriptions include free data (like AGN/Earthbank data) but do not differentiate which portion is free. Moritz Theile suggested that in the gallery, Earthbank data could be marked in green (public), and their commercial data in yellow, while Keith Dimech proposed blue for other people's marketplace data, though Wayne Noble argued against differentiating between their paid data and other sellers' paid data at the initial level.
* Selling Data by Region and Type in the Marketplace Keith Dimech raised a structural concern that they sell data by region and type, while others might sell by package, questioning if this difference is structurally okay. Moritz Theile explained that internally, all data is handled in packages, and a tool exists to split packages into regions to enable selling only a specific region and data type. The consensus reached was that third-party sellers must package their data to be sold by one data type and one region.
* Seller Tool for Data Packaging Wayne Noble proposed, for the future, providing sellers with a "cookie-cutting tool" that automatically splits packages by region or allows them to sell the whole package, simplifying the process for them. This would make the buying experience simple, where the customer only sees the data and not the underlying package structure.
* Addressing Duplicate and Compiled Data in the Marketplace Wayne Noble raised a concern about sellers compiling existing or free data and reselling it in the marketplace, emphasizing the need to be aware of the data being put up for sale. Keith Dimech suggested that everything sold on the marketplace should have an IGSN for standardization and checking. Wayne Noble countered that IGSN minting alone does not prevent duplicate data and emphasized the need for oversight on data quality and content.
* Compilation Data and the Music Industry Model Vinko Novak drew a parallel between data packages and the music industry, comparing a package to a CD compilation where using individual songs (data samples) from other creators requires a license and a share of the revenue. Wayne Noble liked the idea of allowing compilations, comparing it to "Greatest Hits" albums, even if the data already exists, but noted the complexity of tracking shares for public domain data.
* Pricing and Review Process for Commercial Data Fabian Kohlmann stressed that they have the final say on pricing and would review submissions, ensuring they would not approve a price that undercuts their subscription services. Moritz Theile suggested that commercial data packages should have an agreed-upon price tag and a clearly communicated percentage cut for the platform. The process would involve sellers uploading private data, then reaching out to the team to negotiate when they want to make it commercial.
* Future Litho Build Strategy Discussion Keith Dimech introduced the next discussion topic, Litho Build, which requires a strategy for when and how they apply their build capabilities, especially concerning Enterprise projects. They identified key questions for the next meeting, including pricing, capacity consumption, acceptance criteria for future projects, valuable services, and which projects to pursue or shut down.
