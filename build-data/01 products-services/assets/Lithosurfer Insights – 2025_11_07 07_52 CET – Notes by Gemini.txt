Summary
Moritz Theile provided a historical overview of the company's vision, which is to be the "international leading marketplace to buy and sell geological sample based data," and discussed the financial bootstrapping efforts through projects like Earthbank and others, which helped maintain focus on the long-term vision despite pressure to pursue non-core features. While Moritz Theile expressed concerns about exhaustion, limited energy, and growth into new business areas (such as AI consulting and project development) that they believe deviate from the core mission, Wayne Noble and Fabian Kohlmann argued for the necessity of balancing the vision with taking strategic opportunities to maintain viability and secure cash flow. Key disagreements centered on the immediate focus and resources, with Moritz Theile and Keith Dimech emphasizing the urgent need for architectural governance and organizational structure to manage growth, while all participants agreed that leveraging existing tools and securing private exploration data are crucial steps for financing and building the content necessary for the marketplace to succeed.


Details
* Historical Context and Vision Moritz Theile provided a historical overview, tracing the company's origins back six years ago, noting the first email thread with Fabian Kohlmann. They defined the company's vision, crystallized since 2019, as becoming the "international leading marketplace to buy and sell geological sample based data," which they viewed as a revolutionary idea and their guiding compass. Moritz Theile emphasized that having this vision helped set the boundaries for the platform.
* Bootstrapping the Platform The initial challenge was financial, but the company secured significant support from AGM, leading to the Earthbank project, which served to bootstrap the platform by matching requirements with the platform's long-term vision—a strategy referred to as "double dipping". Other projects from organizations like NR Canada and CSRO followed, providing necessary funding while ensuring the platform remained focused on the original vision, despite the hard task of resisting features that didn't fit.
* Current Status and Market Readiness Moritz Theile stated that the company is ready, possessing a strong team, expertise, branding, data, data models, and the platform necessary to fulfill the initial vision. They acknowledged, however, that the actual marketplace is not yet operational, viewing the work up to this point as preparation.
* Differing Views on Future Direction Moritz Theile identified three future possibilities: moving into new business areas (AI consulting, project development), scaling existing business, or building the marketplace. They sensed a difference in opinion, with colleagues favoring growth in existing and new businesses, while their focus was on completing the marketplace, using existing business to finance it.
* Moritz Theile's Energy and Concerns about Growth Moritz Theile expressed being "really tired," with only 10% energy remaining after years of work, and compared their feeling to seeing another mountain range after reaching a summit. They voiced strong opposition to growth into new businesses, stating they don't see the company set up for it and perceived it as an overestimation of the company's current situation. They also expressed concern about wasting time on projects like hardware procurement in Indian cities, which they argued are not the company's core focus and exceed their capabilities.
* Disagreement on Taking Opportunities vs. Maintaining Focus Wayne Noble and Fabian Kohlmann argued that the company must sometimes take opportunities presented to remain viable, even if they differ from the original software vision. Fabian Kohlmann stressed that they do not jump on every opportunity but are pushing for the marketplace, which is close to realization, and that all current building efforts support this goal. Moritz Theile agreed that leveraging data flow through "oil rigs for the data" is the right kind of double dipping.
* Necessity of Cash Flow and Focus on Marketplace Moritz Theile reaffirmed their strong motivation to bring the original vision to fulfillment, even with limited energy, stating a need to bring it to an end and focus. They highlighted the start of the data gallery as the entry point for the new phase. Moritz Theile stated they can commit 50% to 80% of their time to cash flow projects (like AGM and hopefully Amira) while dedicating the remaining time to the marketplace.
* Role of Tool Development and AI in the Vision Fabian Kohlmann pointed out that the company has built an amazing tool, which is valuable and unique, and should be leveraged to finance the path to the marketplace. Moritz Theile expressed the need to differentiate the use of AI, suggesting it should be used for internal tools and agents to ease work and enhance data flow, rather than becoming a new business area or distracting from the core market goal.
* Transitioning Roles and Architectural Governance Moritz Theile suggested they might need to give up the director role due to the tension between the focus on the marketplace and the demands of company growth. They emphasized the critical need for architectural governance to be in capable hands to protect the integrity of the Lithosurfer platform and prevent its ruin through overloading or bad design decisions, asserting this is a major risk when considering Lithodat's growth.
* The Importance of Content and Data for the Marketplace Fabian Kohlmann stressed the need for content in the marketplace, emphasizing that data is crucial for selling. They and Moritz Theile agreed that the data team needs to keep building content independently without distracting core development resources, as an empty marketplace will not succeed.
* Concerns About Growth and Structure Moritz Theile expressed nervousness that the company is "onboarding more and more people without having business" leading to an "emergency situation" where they might have to take every project, which they found intimidating. Keith Dimech noted that the company lacks structure, which causes the founders to be overworked, and suggested that implementing structure and defining roles and responsibilities is essential for managing growth and allowing people to focus on their core tasks.
* Market Value of Data and Strategy for Attraction Wayne Noble questioned the market value of the data, suggesting it might become free due to AI in the coming years, which Moritz Theile acknowledged as potentially breaking the whole vision. Keith Dimech differentiated between academic data (which has limited commercial value) and expensive private mining exploration data, proposing that the strategy should be to attract private data by showcasing Lithodat's data analysis capabilities, and then enabling sales through the marketplace.
* Achieving the Marketplace Vision Moritz Theile and Keith Dimech agreed that the marketplace needs to be built with a dual approach: making it an attractive platform for data utilization (as Keith suggested) while also establishing a point where the first quotes are asked for and sellers realize the benefit of offering their data. Moritz Theile stated that they hope to push the marketplace forward and sell existing products like Lithosurfer licenses to secure cash flow.
* Product Strategy and Monetization Keith Dimech advocated for immediate steps to monetize the current product, suggesting that the product with minor UX adjustments and a "pro" label could attract 15 exploration companies by February. They proposed that the initial focus should be on small exploration companies, using them as trials before targeting bigger players. Keith Dimech also stressed that the team's time should be spent on activities that enable the company's vision, such as securing five or six initial customers, which would then open doors to larger companies like Glen Core.
* Data Collection Priorities Keith Dimech advised against tasking the Mexican team with collecting academic data unless there is a clear mandate for specific data, such as for the Laramides. Instead, they suggested the team should focus on integrating free data from sources like Geoscience Victoria, the University of Queensland, and Geoscience of WA, possibly by building new portals for these organizations for free. Fabian Kohlmann confirmed that the team is currently collecting geochem data from all Scandinavian surveys and that the Australian surveys are next on the list, aiming to consolidate large datasets rather than disjointed information.
* Moritz Theile's Burnout and Role Keith Dimech expressed concern for Moritz Theile's well-being and offered support if they needed to reduce their hours, stating that the dynamic between the three directors is crucial for the business. Moritz Theile confirmed that they are experiencing low energy and heading in the direction of burnout, noting that some of their feelings are a result of this state. Moritz Theile also highlighted that every single business aspect is currently based on Lithu Surfer, which they identified as not a positive situation.
* Next Steps for Direction and Feasibility Keith Dimech requested that the three directors choose a strategic stream for the product and that they and Moritz Theile would then sit down to determine its feasibility, map it out, and create a pathway to the marketplace. Wayne Noble noted that a strong distinction must be made between the Lithu Surfer vision and the company, which they believe are becoming quite different but can coexist. Fabian Kohlmann and Wayne Noble agreed to take the Utopia quiz as requested by Keith Dimech, which is intended to lead into the structure needed for the marketplace.
