Nov 12, 2025
Lithodat Product Meeting
Invited Keith Dimech Fabian Kohlmann Wayne Noble Vinko Novak Moritz Theile
Attachments Lithodat Product Meeting 
Meeting records Recording 


Summary
Keith Dimech, Moritz Theile, Vinko Novak, Fabian Kohlmann, and Wayne Noble discussed a new three-tiered commercial structure for "Lithu Surfer" (Free, Pro, and Enterprise), generally agreeing on the model but raising concerns about the operational, legal, and development complexities of managing licenses, data liability, and feature differentiation, particularly for the Pro tier. Key areas of focus included defining the feature sets for the Free and Pro licenses, which needs clarification from Wayne Noble, and ensuring the Enterprise license provides bespoke, value-added services like data cleaning and custom branding to lock in customers. The team also explored the system's vision for data separation for large companies, organizational terminology, and a future sample planning tool, concluding that the necessary Minimum Viable Product development mainly centers on user experience changes ahead of a conference showcase in January.


Details
* Proposed Commercial Structure and Product Tiers Keith Dimech introduced a proposal for a three-tiered commercial structure consisting of "Lithu Surfer" (software), "Litha" (data), and "Litha Build". The primary focus was on Lithu Surfer, which would be broken down into a free version, a per-user "Lithu Pro" subscription with more features, and a customized "Lithu Surfer Enterprise" offering. The key questions for the meeting were to agree on the structure, assess implementation feasibility with the current setup, and determine if it's worthwhile.
* General Agreement and Necessary Operational Details The participants generally agreed in principle with the three-tier subscription model for Lithu Surfer. However, Moritz Theile raised the need for clarification on operational details, especially regarding what happens after a contract is signed and how to ensure the team can provide the defined licenses. Vinko Novak concurred, emphasizing the need to build the operational mechanisms to switch users between free and Pro licenses and track subscriptions.
* Legal and Development Concerns Concerns were raised regarding legal and development requirements necessary to support the new structure, especially when handling private data for the Pro offering, which would necessitate new Terms and Conditions or contracts. Vinko Novak noted that the idea of a premium model, where new users start free and upgrade to Pro after learning the platform, is a good idea.
* Definition of the Third Tier (Pro License) Fabian Kohlmann questioned what Keith Dimech meant by the "third tier," to which Keith Dimech clarified that it refers to the addition of a per-person subscription seat model, which does not currently exist, as the current structure is essentially Free and Enterprise. Wayne Noble suggested that the main issue is determining what features should be bundled into each tier to justify the pricing, noting that the free version is the crucial basis for making the paid tiers attractive.
* Free Version Features and Limitations Keith Dimech suggested that the free version should include current features such as access to AGN data and all tools on the left-hand side toolbar, but exclude advanced features like chemistry dashboards. Moritz Theile advised that the current free license is adequate and that the focus should be on selling what they currently have, avoiding changes to existing free features unless absolutely necessary to sell the new products. Wayne Noble agreed that rolling back features currently free in EarthBank, which acts as a free version, would be perceived negatively.
* Differentiating Free and Paid Tiers Moritz Theile clarified that the current free version is very restricted, primarily limiting users to only sample data and lacking teamwork, data sharing, publishing, and support, making it unsuitable for "serious use". Fabian Kohlmann added that Litho Surfer Pro could include extra tools and functionalities like special cooling plots and custom WMS feeds. Vinko Novak suggested that the free version could remove personalization, losing all searches and data views upon log out, which Wayne Noble confirmed is technically feasible as preferences are currently browser-based.
* Pro Features Attractive to Mining Companies In the Pro tier, Moritz Theile listed features appealing to mining companies, including a support package, an onboarding workshop, API documentation and development support, and a Service Level Agreement. Keith Dimech reiterated the importance of the ability to put in their own data, API access, and support. Wayne Noble added the possibility of using a Geo server for their own layers and extending features like the paleo tool.
* Data Sharing and Team Functionality Moritz Theile explained that on the free license, users uploading data cannot share it with others, as it remains private. They suggested preventing free users from creating teams to avoid bypassing this restriction. Keith Dimech affirmed that the ability for teams to add and view shared data is a powerful and necessary functionality for Pro users, particularly in exploration scenarios.
* Data Upload Limits and Costs The team confirmed that they currently cannot limit data upload amounts technically, but could monitor usage and apply limits through the Terms and Conditions (T&Cs). Moritz Theile noted that the ability to set limits and charge for storage would allow the Pro license to be scalable. Vinko Novak raised a concern about preventing the same data from being imported multiple times by different users, which is not currently detected.
* Data Management for Users Vinko Novak confirmed that users can logically delete their own data, with the option for physical deletion. The ability for Pro users to download all their uploaded data in a structured way was proposed as a valuable feature, which Moritz Theile confirmed would only return the CSV file they uploaded. Vinko Novak departed from the meeting early to attend another commitment.
* DOI Minting, Support, and Training DOI minting is a configurable feature that can be turned on or off per institution. Moritz Theile indicated that DOI minting is an expense for the company, suggesting a limit based on cost per mint, which could be offered as an add-on or credit-based system. Structured support and training, including workshops, are already available, which Fabian Kohlmann estimated would take about three hours to cover all training modules.
* Enterprise License Features and Pricing Model Keith Dimech defined the Enterprise license as a contractual, bespoke offering that goes beyond the standardized Pro license. Enterprise features would include work-intensive services like a custom branded portal, data cleaning, ingestion templates for CSVs, and hosting. The Enterprise model is less scalable than the Pro license due to the high amount of personalized effort required, but it is expected to lock in customers for recurring annual revenue.
* Finalizing Pro Feature List and Timing Keith Dimech requested that Wayne Noble provide a list of the actual names of features currently behind licenses, including a description, to clearly define the free and paid tiers. Wayne Noble suggested a target timeline of mid-January for having the new structure ready to present at an upcoming conference. Fabian Kohlmann and Moritz Theile agreed on the value of hosting a full Litho Surfer training session for all staff, including new employees and non-geoscientists, to ensure everyone understands the system's capabilities.
* Project Timeline and Test Users Wayne Noble emphasized the importance of completing the project by January. Keith Dimech agreed, noting that the start would involve teething issues and that they would need to engage test users and gather test cases.
* Enterprise License Structure and Value Fabian Kohlmann clarified that the Enterprise license would be an extra cost for custom builds. Moritz Theile questioned the need for the Enterprise license versus a new development project, but Keith Dimech and Fabian Kohlmann explained it as a discounted bundle of the Listener Pro package with included services like data cleaning to bind customers and create dependencies.
* Pricing and Locking in Enterprise Customers Keith Dimech provided a hypothetical pricing structure, suggesting that Enterprise might be more cost-effective for companies with 11 or more users, with the goal of locking them in for potential added cleaning services. Keith Dimech noted that revenue from these services could directly support the Mexican team's operations. Fabian Kohlmann suggested limiting the scope of initial data cleaning services.
* Training and Customer Retention Wayne Noble argued that providing training is advantageous because it is not difficult for them and helps keep customers engaged with the system, increasing the likelihood of retention. Keith Dimech highlighted that a key differentiator for their company is offering PhD geologists to look at and clean the data, which would be exclusive to the Enterprise license holders.
* Enterprise Services and Branding Fabian Kohlmann suggested offering discounts for other services like litho data or mine services to Enterprise customers. Moritz Theile proposed a branded portal with a custom URL as a key feature differentiating Enterprise from Pro, and Keith Dimech added the potential for Single Sign-On (SSO).
* Single Sign-On (SSO) Implementation Keith Dimech stated that some companies, like their own (John Holland), will not purchase products without SSO. The team discussed implementing SSO, with Keith Dimech suggesting they launch Enterprise without it and add it when a customer needs it and pays for the setup. Moritz Theile confirmed that SSO is possible and could be a feature turned on or off at the user or instance level.
* Clarifying Organizational Schema Keith Dimech sought clarification on the difference between organization, institution, and community. Moritz Theile explained that institution was chosen as a term to suit both universities and companies, while organization might be more suitable for industry, and a community is defined as a group of institutions under a single contract, for example, multiple institutions bound by one EarthBank contract.
* Community and Institution Relationships Fabian Kohlmann brought up the example of Ammyra as a community where members from different institutions (like BHP or Rio Tinto) sign in with their work emails. Moritz Theile confirmed that an institution can be part of multiple communities, which follows an end-to-end relationship model. Moritz Theile clarified that a user assigned to an institution within a community gets features bought by either the institution or the community.
* Enterprise License Model for Large Companies Keith Dimech questioned whether an Enterprise license for a company like BHP should be set up at the community or institute level. Wayne Noble suggested setting it up at the institute level, but if BHP wanted different internal groups to operate separately, the countries or departments could be institutes under BHP as a community. Moritz Theile provided an example, suggesting that for BHP, the entity with the contract could be the BHP community, and sub-institutions could have different rights or features.
* Renaming Organizational Terminology Fabian Kohlmann pointed out that their current names are very academic and suggested using terms like organizations instead of institutions and parent company or group instead of community when selling to industry. Keith Dimech acknowledged this as a good point.
* Enterprise Portal Branding and URLs Keith Dimech asked about the Enterprise portal's URL, questioning if it should be a CNAME or if the customer owns the URL. Moritz Theile suggested offering customers their own URL to give them more ownership, while Fabian Kohlmann raised security concerns about using original company domains, noting different cybersecurity requirements. Keith Dimech suggested CNAMEs or a subdomain like `litho.bhp.com` as defaults, acknowledging that fully embedded URLs would incur extra costs for cyber hardening.
* Data Access and Distribution Levels Keith Dimech inquired if multiple institutes within a community could share data or if they could block data access. Moritz Theile explained that they have five distribution states (private, public, community, institution, and commercial), and by setting the distribution level, users within the institution or community can see the data. Keith Dimech emphasized the need for institutional separation to handle classified information, allowing teams within a large company like BHP to control data sharing.
* Adapters to Existing Systems Fabian Kohlmann suggested offering adapters or plugins to existing industry data management systems like Acquire or Geobank as part of, or an add-on to, the Enterprise license to make selling easier. Keith Dimech agreed to list this as an additional tool to be built, but not necessarily as a standard part of the Enterprise agreement.
* Ammyra Data Integration and Partnership Keith Dimech raised the possibility of selling Ammyra's data alongside customer data through their platform. Fabian Kohlmann clarified that Ammyra data is for their members in a different database, suggesting it would be more beneficial for them if they could preview their data through Ammyra to engage mining companies.
* Multi-Department and Multi-Institution Support as a Selling Point Moritz Theile emphasized that multi-department and multi-institution support should be highlighted as a key benefit of the Enterprise license, as it addresses the need for different departments to manage their own budgets and data. Moritz Theile explained that a user with the Pro license would have all departments see all data, making the Enterprise license a necessary upgrade for data separation.
* UX Changes for Different Licenses The team discussed necessary UX changes, noting that the Pro version should have some visible branding or features that make it feel different from the free version. Keith Dimech noted that the package upload feature for Pro users needs to be as easy as possible. Wayne Noble suggested generating scenarios with real customers to help define the UX.
* Customer Journey from Free to Pro Moritz Theile outlined the typical customer journey, starting with free registration and then encountering limitations (like inability to upload geochemical data), which prompts them to ask about Pro licenses. Fabian Kohlmann added that other valuable features should be locked behind the paywall to entice users to upgrade.
* Automating License Upgrade and Purchase Hurdles Fabian Kohlmann suggested future automation for upgrading from the free to Pro license, possibly through a simple Stripe purchase. Keith Dimech cautioned that instant purchases for higher costs might be difficult, suggesting they aim for Purchase Order (PO) level procurement, while noting the contractual hurdles required for data liability.
* Free Version as a Restricted Pro Version Keith Dimech suggested that the free version should be a locked, restricted version of Pro, with everything available but many features locked, displaying pop-ups indicating Pro or Enterprise license requirements. Moritz Theile agreed, confirming that the Enterprise features, like the custom portal and multi-department capability, should be prominent upsells.
* Customer Service for Subscription Model Keith Dimech emphasized the critical need for excellent customer service for the subscription model, suggesting a designated customer relationship manager and efficient support processes.
* Focus on Data Types and Regions Keith Dimech stressed the importance of simplifying the filtering UX for free, Pro, and Enterprise users by focusing on data types and regions rather than packages. Moritz Theile and Fabian Kohlmann agreed but cautioned against renaming the core concept of package to project internally for coherency, as users can simply name their packages by their project name.
* User Roles and License Management Wayne Noble suggested adding a new user role for Pro users to drive UI changes, but Moritz Theile countered that it would be better managed by having a license feature, such as a "pro style" feature, assigned to the institution. Moritz Theile explained that assigning the predefined Pro license to the institution would instantly upgrade the user experience.
* Grouping of Packages The team identified the ability to group packages together as a high-priority, desirable feature, although not vital for the MVP. Wayne Noble noted that it would fit well with the existing data hierarchies.
* Exploration Planner Feature Keith Dimech introduced the idea of a potential future feature where Pro users could plan their exploration jobs in the app, creating samples that would be automatically assigned data once the lab results arrive. Moritz Theile confirmed that the underlying functionality for this (sample creation, location data) mostly exists, but the user experience would need refinement, possibly involving creating a CSV from the app rather than uploading one.
* Exploration Planning and Sample Taking Tool Keith Dimech proposed a feature for planning exploration, particularly for drilling holes and taking samples, suggesting it could start as a training module and evolve into an automated feature. Moritz Theile clarified that this would be a separate tool embedded in the system, one that outputs a sample input file for the main system, which constitutes an entire project requiring substantial development. Keith Dimech confirmed that a rudimentary version of this tool that could be added to the current system was what they were asking for.
* Development Priority and Strategy Moritz Theile emphasized that adding a whole new tool would require attention and resources, cautioning against such decisions unless it is a necessary feature for securing system purchases. Wayne Noble suggested that defining screen flows would facilitate discussing what is needed once a decision is made to pursue the feature. Fabian Kohlmann stressed focusing on selling the current offerings first, but agreed the proposed feature is a great idea that should be scoped out for potential future upselling.
* System Vision and Sample Management Keith Dimech discussed the future vision of better connection with labs, where sample names and IDs link directly from planning to the Certificate of Conformity (COC) and back to the platform upon analysis. Moritz Theile confirmed this aligns with the system's core vision, where samples have a different lifecycle from the data, enabling users to plan sample spots, import the file, and download DOIs for labeling before analysis, which allows the lab to upload data directly to the platform for connection. Keith Dimech found this design particularly useful for environmental sampling, which often involves repeated sampling of the same location.
* Focus on MVP and Development Work Keith Dimech confirmed the sample planning tool is a future consideration and sought to finalize the discussion on the Minimum Viable Product (MVP) before client sign-up. The consensus was that the main development work needed is focused on UX changes for the system. Moritz Theile noted that based on the current discussions, they are very close to being able to secure a signature for a pro license.
* Upcoming Showcases and Marketing Strategy Wayne Noble planned to showcase the current state of the system at a conference on January 15th to gather feedback from various groups like big surveys, individuals, and mining companies. Fabian Kohlmann highlighted the need to have the system fully tuned by then, particularly for an interview, focusing on selling it to junior and mid-tier companies by emphasizing that it enables them to use exploration tools previously available only to majors. Keith Dimech committed to listing necessary UX changes and developing branding copy for a two-page catalog by the end of the year so Wayne Noble can present it in January.
* Testing, Website Rebrand, and Copywriting Keith Dimech planned to find test users from Lab West's clients by December or January/February to try out the new features for free for three to six months to learn from real data. Wayne Noble added that the website rebrand, including potentially Spanish and EU sites, needs to be done along with UX changes, and they have designs ready if they receive the necessary concepts and text. Keith Dimech emphasized the importance of external advice for website wording and UX to ensure the messaging is clear to the layperson and suggested getting feedback from geology friends before the website upgrade.
* Agenda for the Next Meeting on Commercial Data Keith Dimech outlined the agenda for the next meeting, which will focus on commercial data, including "lithodata free," "lithodata exchange" (marketplace for third parties), and "lithodata private" (which Moritz Theile later clarified as "institutional data"). Key questions include how data is collected, estimating prices for subscription models based on data types and regions, how to implement the commercial marketplace (e.g., uploading packages, handling multiple regions), and commission decisions.
* Data Quality and Security in the Marketplace The discussion also needs to cover data quality verification for third-party data sold on the platform, including the possibility of a "blue tick" or verification model. The team discussed whether to allow anyone to sell data and the responsibility for data quality, with Fabian Kohlmann suggesting they are responsible for correct implementation but not the actual data. Moritz Theile and Wayne Noble agreed on the need for a ranking system and that they should not knowingly allow "horrible" data, suggesting the sellers need to be verified institutions or companies. Keith Dimech agreed to change the terminology from "private data" to "institutional data" for clarity.
* Preparation and Scheduling of Next Meeting Moritz Theile suggested that they need to align on topics and details, similar to the current preparation, before the next meeting to ensure a productive discussion. The next meeting was scheduled for Monday at 6 o'clock.
