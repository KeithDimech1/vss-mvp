Call for Pre-Qualification
The Saudi Geological Survey (SGS) intends to implement a new Advanced
Analytics Platform project as a first step in the development of the new Saudi
Center for Geoscience Data Analytics (GDAC-SA), which is prominent among the
objectives of Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 as it seeks to diversify the Kingdom's
economy through the mineral resources sector. The Saudi Geological Survey
(SGS) is pleased to invite you to participate in the pre-qualification process for
the Advanced Analytics Platform project of the Saudi Center for Geological
Science Data Analysis (GDAC-SA).
The purpose of this RFQ is to assess the capabilities of potential bidders for the Saudi
Geoscience Data Analytics Center's AI platform. Qualified bidders will then be invited to
move from the RFP stage to the RFP stage. Due to the complex nature of the RFP/RFP
process, bidders can apply in consortia to meet all the requirements.
The SGS invites you to submit Request for Qualifications (RFQ) to complete the
qualification requirements and participate in the bidding process for this project. In order
for the application to be considered, the application must meet all the required elements
contained in this qualification application form and submit all required documents and
certificates, as any lack of documentation may result in the disqualification of the
application.

Section One: Introduction
Introduction:
In April 2016, the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia officially announced the "Saudi Vision 2030"
(hereinafter referred to as "Vision 2030") and the "National Transformation Plan", which aspires
to diversify the Saudi economy, with digitalization and artificial intelligence as the most important
elements of this transformation. As Saudi Arabia strives to be among the top 15 countries in AI,
among the top 10 countries with open-source data, and among the top 20 contributors to
research and innovation globally by 2030, Saudi Arabia's development goals are to achieve its
economic transformation, in part, through the further development of the big data and artificial
intelligence industries.
The Saudi Geological Survey is one of the entities of the Ministry of Industry and Mineral
Resources, which is the main entity for the creation and storage of geological data and providing
access to it in the Kingdom, as the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia in general has not yet been
sufficiently explored in terms of solid minerals, and there are many untapped opportunities in
the mining sector in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, as the Arabian Shield area located within the
Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, which extends over an area of 600,000 square kilometers, is
considered a major source of precious and essential minerals. More than 90 species and at
least 15 of them are commercially viable.

The Saudi Geological Survey has received approval to establish a Geological Data Analysis
Center that will be powered by an AI/Big Data Analytics Platform. The platform will enable the
transformation of data and analytics related to geological exploration as well as the
environmental and social impacts associated with mining, and the Geological Data Analysis
Center will also develop geoscientific laboratories to produce the data required for the analytical
requirements of the center. A new research and training institute will be established to be a
catalyst for pioneering research and building capacity within the Kingdom, and a state-of-theart facility dedicated to this purpose will be built on the Geological Survey campus.

Vision of the Geological Data Analysis Center Platform
The Geological Data Analysis Center should primarily support mineral exploration as a core objective of Vision 2030, and mineral
exploration will be considered as the main purpose and product of the Center. As shown in Figure 2, the AI platform must be able to
grow and evolve to keep pace with the broad research interests of the Geological Data Analysis Center in order to advance the
research and AI goals in Saudi Arabia.

The AI platform must be able to perform data transformations related to exploration geology
and its analysis, assess the environmental and social impact of mining, and the analytics
platform provider must be able to deliver and enable world-class AI and analytics applications.
The first and second phases of the development of the Geological Data Analysis Center and
its capabilities will be related to the integration of geological data, mineral exploration and
prospecting, as the identification of high-potential mineral resources that are often hidden and
extremely deep makes the field of exploration a highly complex science because of its diverse
methods and data, including reports, geochemistry, geophysics, remote sensing, geology,
drilling records, etc., in addition to many analytical features, such as the possibility of the
presence of minerals, rocks, and structures. and variable metals, and others. Each type of data
and every geological feature helps tell a possible story of what the Earth is hiding. However,
the primary goal of mineral exploration analytics is the ability to aggregate this data, normalize
it and model multiple important features as they are likely to be productive from a given
geographic location.

The first phase: the proof of concept phase
To achieve the goals of the Geological Data Analysis Center for Mineral Exploration, the AI
platform will be developed by integrating a wide range of data types, creating engineering
features from different forms of data. Most of this data will be at the national and regional levels
and contain aggregations of data and gaps, including structured and unstructured data, some
of which are without metadata including the locations of this data which is mostly obtained from
the National Geological Database (NGD) and the National Information Center (NIC). The phase
will require the Proponent also has the ability to integrate complex features from different types
of data – such as natural language processing, image analysis, and/or the development of new
algorithms. A large amount of new geological data is currently being collected, such as detailed
geological maps and geochemistry, which will be part of the required data conversion. The
Proponent that is subsequently awarded the scope of a specific area within the Kingdom to
implement an initial pilot project for exploratory forecasting. Upon successful completion of the

The Second Phase of the Artificial Intelligence Platform at the Saudi Center
for Geological Data Analysis: Exploration Prospects Phase
The second step is designed to support the Saudi Geological Survey (SGS) and the Geological
Data Analysis Center (GDAC) by providing them with advanced AI-based tools to enhance their
capabilities to perform advanced geological analyses at the level of resources, commodities or
sediment types. To achieve this goal, the platform must include a range of ready-made
analytical methods, as well as capabilities that allow for the rapid development of new analysis
methods, as well as the ability to build advanced taxonomic models that mimic mineral systems
It identifies important characteristics for the discovery of new deposits.

Phase III of the Artificial Intelligence Platform of the Saudi Center for
Geological Data Analysis: Integration of Geoscience Data
As part of the vision of the Geological Data Analysis Center, the Saudi Geological Survey aims
to create an AI platform that can continue to grow and expand to cover other geoscience-related
implications, and as in Phase 1, Phase 3 will focus on creating a more expansive data

Phase IV: Cumulative Impact Analysis
Like most complex analytical challenges, changes to the natural environment from various
factors have intentional and unintended effects on human, environmental, and economic
systems, and these effects rarely come from just one source, and it is often other factors such
as time, duration, concentration, and a combination of factors that cause the effects on complex
systems. Cumulative effects research typically involves monitoring and analyzing multiple
variables over time to assess how different factors interact and the changes they bring about
in the ecosystem under study. This can include studying changes in the built environment,
biodiversity, ecosystem function, water quality and other indicators of ecosystem health. By
understanding how different factors interact and accumulate over time, cumulative impact
research can help make decisions about how to manage and mitigate the impact of human
activities on the environment. More detailed information will be provided during the RFP
process.

Integration of Stages 1-4
Enabling Saudi Arabia's strategy to develop the mining sector as a third economic pillar is a
strategic necessity centered on the capabilities of the Geological Data Analysis Center,
particularly through its AI platform. The four components of the development of the AI platform
of the Saudi Center for Geoscience Data Analysis are formed through four primary objectives:
developing the analytical model by type of commodity or mineral resources, generating new
targets, developing extracted resources and environmental assessment of high-potential
resources of them.

Section Two: General Conditions
The purpose of the Request for Qualifications (RFQ) is to provide the opportunity for
companies or consortiums with experience and qualification to implement the Advanced
Analytics Platform project of the Saudi Center for Geological Science Data Analysis.
Eligible companies are invited to submit their bids (financial and technical) for the Saudi
Center for Geoscience Data Analysis (GDAC-SA) Advanced Analytics Platform briefly
described here based on the Proposal and Specification Booklet (RFP), which will be sent to
the qualified bidders.
The bidder can be either a single company/entity or a group of interested and qualified entities.
In the case of an alliance, qualification applications must be submitted to all members of that
alliance, including all partners and subcontractors. To be considered for the qualification process,
the bidder must provide the following:
1. A brief statement of the company/entity, its history and any related projects
implemented in the last five years.
2. Formal agreements between trade alliance partners as well as subcontractors.

Award Mechanism
In order to ensure clarity and transparency in the procurement process, it is important to
determine the mechanism by which contracts are awarded at the end of the competition
process, to this end, this Request for Qualifications (RFQ) serves as the initial stage of a
two-tier selection process. During the evaluation of qualification submissions, bidders who
meet and exceed the established qualification criteria for the subsequent stage—the
Request for Proposals (RFP)—will be notified. The RFP stage provides an opportunity for
the selected bidders to submit additional details, and serves as a more in-depth
exploration of each bidder’s capabilities and proposed solutions, thereby enabling a
comprehensive assessment to ensure the selection of the most suitable candidate for
contract award. Accordingly, participation in the RFP stage is contingent upon successfully
passing the qualification requirements during this RFQ stage..
